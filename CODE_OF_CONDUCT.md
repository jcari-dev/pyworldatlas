# Community code of conduct

## Our standard

PyWorldAtlas is built for education, curiosity, and respectful collaboration.
Everyone participating in the project is expected to treat other people and
communities with dignity, patience, and good faith.

Positive participation includes:

- Discussing data, sources, and code without attacking people.
- Welcoming questions from learners and contributors of every background.
- Giving factual corrections with clear evidence and respectful language.
- Acknowledging uncertainty and the limits of one's own perspective.
- Protecting private information and avoiding public escalation of sensitive
  conduct reports.

Unacceptable participation includes:

- Hatred, dehumanization, harassment, threats, or discriminatory language.
- Personal attacks, intimidation, stalking, or unwanted sexual attention.
- Derogatory generalizations about a nationality, ethnicity, race, religion,
  disability, gender, sexual orientation, or other personal identity.
- Using a data or naming disagreement to promote hostility.
- Deliberately publishing private information or encouraging abuse.
- Repeated disruption after a maintainer has asked for a discussion to stop.

## Scope

This code applies to repository issues, pull requests, discussions,
documentation, examples, review records, and project-related public spaces. It
also applies when someone officially represents the project.

Technical disagreement is welcome. Criticism must address the field, source,
method, or implementation rather than the identity or motives of another
person.

## Reporting and enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove content, close discussions, reject contributions,
or restrict participation when behavior conflicts with this code. Responses
should be proportionate, documented where appropriate, and focused on restoring
a safe working environment.

Do not place private or sensitive details in a public issue. Report serious
abuse through GitHub's private reporting tools or contact the repository owner
privately through their GitHub profile. Immediate threats should be reported to
the appropriate local service.

Good-faith reports and corrections must not result in retaliation.
